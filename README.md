# Customer Satisfaction & CRM Analysis | Coop Case Study

**Academic group project | Customer Analytics · Market Research · CRM**

**Tools:** R, Qualtrics  
**Methods:** Survey Analysis, Importance–Performance Analysis (IPA), Factor Analysis, Regression Analysis

## Project Overview

This academic case study examines factors associated with customer satisfaction in Coop supermarkets in Italy. The project combines survey data and statistical analysis to identify satisfaction drivers, compare customer groups and explore potential priorities for retail customer experience and customer relationship management (CRM).

## Objectives

- Assess satisfaction with different aspects of the shopping experience.
- Identify service attributes that customers consider particularly important.
- Compare the priorities of Coop members and non-members.
- Examine associations between customer experience dimensions and overall satisfaction.

## Data & Methodology

The analysis was based on **181 survey responses**, with data collection and analysis supported by **Qualtrics** and **R**.

1. **Descriptive analysis:** Examined satisfaction and perceived importance for attributes including product quality, staff, accessibility, store layout, checkout and membership benefits.
2. **Importance–Performance Analysis (IPA):** Compared perceived importance and satisfaction to highlight potential improvement priorities.
3. **Factor analysis:** Identified five interpretable dimensions of customer experience, supported by internal consistency checks using Cronbach's alpha.
4. **Regression analysis:** Assessed associations between customer experience dimensions and overall satisfaction.
5. **Group comparisons:** Examined differences between Coop members and non-members.

## Key Findings

- **Store layout and products** were the strongest predictors of overall satisfaction in the regression model reported in the final presentation.
- **Membership programme factors** were another relevant dimension of satisfaction.
- The regression model reported in the presentation achieved an **adjusted R² of 0.45**.
- Respondents generally expressed positive views of product quality, assortment and shelf organisation.
- **Members and non-members had different priorities:** non-members placed greater emphasis on promotions, while members highlighted more concerns about personnel and product availability.
- Different analytical approaches suggested partly different improvement priorities, showing the importance of interpreting findings in context.

## Business Implications

The case study demonstrates how customer analytics can support retail decisions by identifying satisfaction drivers, comparing customer groups and suggesting areas for service and CRM improvement.

The results are **exploratory and descriptive**: they do not establish causal effects and should not be interpreted as representative of all Coop customers.
